01 / The concept

Two sides.
One conversation.

Our team is developing a two-sided device designed to make everyday conversations easier between Deaf and hearing people.

The goal is to translate American Sign Language captured by a camera into readable text, while converting spoken responses into text for the person signing.

Communication in both directions

ASL → readable text

Camera-captured signing is the input for the recognition work.

Speech → readable text

Spoken responses are converted into text for the person signing.

The two-way device is under development.
02 / My contributions

Making movement
easier to inspect.

My work focuses on software for the product: building a tool that visualizes signing movements through animated points and lines, and working on AI models for reading signs.

Software developmentMovement visualizationSign-recognition AI

The visualization tool

The tool represents hand positions, finger movements, facial expressions, and body motion. It helps our team inspect the movement data used in sign language recognition.

Making these movements visible gives us a way to examine what the data is capturing while developing the recognition software.

03 / Understanding the movement data

More than a hand position.

The visualization brings different parts of signing movement into one view so the team can inspect them together.

Supporting recognition development

I’m also working on AI models for reading signs. The visualization and model work are part of our ongoing exploration of how software can support more accessible communication.
